# Factual and historical integrity

Read this reference whenever real-world facts materially constrain the story.
Historical integrity is independent of genre: a romance, thriller, comedy, or
fantasy may still require this workflow.

## Evidence ledger

For each consequential claim, record its source and classify it as:

- verified fact;
- probable or source-supported inference;
- disputed or source-conflicted;
- explicit artistic reconstruction;
- invention or counterfactual premise.

Prefer primary records and authoritative scholarship. Distinguish the date an
event happened from the date a source was published. Do not repeat a detail
across secondary summaries as though repetition made it independent evidence.

## Honest dramatization

Never present invented private thoughts, exact dialogue, motives, chronology,
or eyewitness detail as documented fact. Reconstructed dialogue may convey
verified circumstances, but must not reverse known positions or manufacture a
decisive act. Preserve uncertainty when sources conflict.

For living people, sensitive subjects, atrocities, children, death, or real
victims, avoid sensational framing and unsupported moral judgment. Record
material visual reconstructions as well as textual ones.

In auto-approve mode, a low-impact gap may be filled only as an explicit
reconstruction consistent with known constraints. Stop for ambiguity that
would change responsibility, causality, identity, chronology, or the meaning
of a real person's actions.

## Delivery

Keep the evidence ledger with the story's authoring documentation when the
current repository contract provides a location. The final handoff must state
the material reconstructions and unresolved disputes without overstating
certainty.
