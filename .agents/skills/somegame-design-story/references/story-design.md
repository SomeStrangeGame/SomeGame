# Story design

Read this reference for every new story.

## Author brief

Record the author's own genre wording verbatim. Keep it distinct from factual
basis. Also establish audience, tone, themes, prohibited material, expected
play time, linear or branching form, and the desired degree of player agency.
Ask only for missing decisions that materially change the result.

Genre is never inferred. In auto-approve mode, scope and ordinary creative
details may be inferred, but the author must still supply the genre.

## Narrative package

Create a compact package before implementation:

- logline and promise to the player;
- synopsis and central conflict;
- setting rules and continuity constraints;
- cast with goals, relationships, and emotional arcs;
- scene sequence with location, time, dramatic purpose, participants, costume,
  emotion, player choice, consequence, and required media;
- ending conditions and state that must survive choices.

Choices should express meaningful perspective, risk, relationship, knowledge,
or consequence. Do not add cosmetic choices solely to increase branch count.
Prefer reconvergence when branches do not justify duplicated art and prose.

## Checkpoints

In guided mode, obtain approval for the narrative package before full prose and
for the scene/asset manifest before art production. In auto-approve mode, keep
the same artifacts as reviewable evidence without pausing unless a stopping
condition from `SKILL.md` occurs.

## Originality-review loop

Treat the first completed narrative package as iteration 1. Run no more than
five reviews total; a revision made after review N is evaluated by review N+1.
Do not perform extra hidden iterations after review 5.

For each review:

1. Extract compact search fingerprints from the current draft: distinctive
   wording, unusual character/relationship configurations, specific chains of
   events, signature setting devices, choice/consequence structures, and
   endings. Do not upload or reproduce the full unpublished draft merely to
   search it.
2. Search available public sources and any comparison corpus supplied by the
   author. Use exact-phrase searches for distinctive wording and broader
   searches for unusually specific narrative combinations. Record direct source
   links and distinguish inspected sources from search-result snippets.
3. Compare expression and the selection, arrangement, and sequence of concrete
   elements. Shared genre conventions, archetypes, broad premises, historical
   facts, and independently necessary factual chronology are not enough to
   establish material risk.
4. Record each candidate with its matched elements, meaningful differences,
   source, confidence, and `low`, `medium`, or `high` risk. `Medium` and `high`
   findings are material. A `low` finding remains in the log but does not block
   progression.
5. If no material finding remains, mark the gate `passed` and end the loop. If a
   material finding remains and fewer than five reviews have run, define a
   targeted revision for the next iteration and preserve unaffected approved
   decisions.

Targeted revision should change the smallest combination that creates the risk,
not merely replace names or paraphrase suspicious wording. Depending on the
finding, change causal structure, character motivations and relationships,
scene order, player agency, consequences, setting mechanics, or resolution.
Keep an iteration log with the draft fingerprint, findings, changes attempted,
and result so later reviews can see whether the underlying similarity actually
decreased.

Intentional adaptation, licensed material, public-domain sources, and explicit
homage require attribution and rights/provenance handling rather than automatic
rewriting. Report them separately and follow the author's stated basis.

After review 5, any remaining medium or high finding makes the result `blocked`.
Return the source evidence, exact implicated narrative elements, why the risk
survived, and the five-iteration change log. Never downgrade a finding merely to
continue production. If meaningful source search was unavailable or materially
incomplete, state that limitation; absence of discovered matches is not proof of
absolute uniqueness or legal clearance.
