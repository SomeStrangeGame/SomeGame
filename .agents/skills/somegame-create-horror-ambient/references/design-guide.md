# Horror ambient design guide

## Pressure without hum

Use several low-mid partials rather than one dominant oscillator. Slowly vary amplitude, tuning, and stereo phase independently. A useful starting region is 40–180 Hz for weight, with restrained material up to roughly 1.5–3 kHz for texture. High-pass below 25–30 Hz and avoid sustained mains-adjacent buzz.

Favor unresolved intervals and long asymmetrical swells. Soft saturation can add density without raising peak level. Keep attacks several tens of milliseconds or longer unless a startle is explicitly requested.

## Convincing light metal creaks

A structural creak is a sequence of friction catches:

- total gesture around 0.7–1.4 seconds;
- irregular catches roughly 50–150 ms apart;
- each catch excites a short 30–90 ms metallic resonance;
- moderate, inconsistent pitch motion rather than a continuous sweep;
- sparse placement and subdued level.

Long stable resonances become a note. Large smooth pitch bends sound cartoonish. Unfiltered noise sounds like scraping or shuffling.

## Barely intelligible voices

Start from genuine speech. For background menace, use short phrases with restrained delivery and pauses. Keep the direct signal low and diffuse it with several dark delays.

Typical progression from clear to obscure:

1. Lower the voice beneath the bed.
2. Attenuate consonant presence, usually above 1–2.5 kHz.
3. Increase the echo-to-direct relationship with nonuniform delays.
4. Mix a low-level reversed copy to break lexical reconstruction.
5. Only then reduce direct articulation further.

Do not rely on breath noise alone: it removes phonetic information and is perceived as cloth, wind, or footsteps. If words become totally absent, restore a small amount of direct consonant signal.

For drawn-out words, time-stretch the voiced phrase while preserving pitch; inserting punctuation creates pauses but does not lengthen phonemes. For hoarseness, use subtle fast amplitude instability and soft saturation. Heavy bit reduction produces radio/static artifacts rather than a throat-like rasp.

## Mixing and safety

Keep layers as separate stems until final approval. Use gentle compression and a limiter with headroom. Avoid sudden peaks, piercing bands, and excessive sub-bass unless explicitly requested. Validate with both headphones and a small speaker when available because low-level voices can disappear on one playback system and become obvious on another.
